# What the online weather sources return

The measurements here date from 2026-09-27, when the rain logging was designed.  The
question was whether Open-Meteo, or OpenWeatherMap as an alternative, can give a rain
total since midnight that looks only backwards in time and that describes something
observed.  CumulusMX is covered separately in `cumulusmx-json-api.md`.

## Open-Meteo sends model output

Open-Meteo's own documentation says "Current conditions are based on 15-minutely
weather model data."  Its Historical Forecast API is built by "stitching the first
hours of each successive model run", and the page says each run starts from real
measurements.  Its Historical Weather API serves reanalysis, which "uses a combination
of weather station, aircraft, buoy, radar, and satellite observations", and arrives
five days late for ERA5.  In a GitHub discussion on 2025-02-06 (open-meteo/open-meteo
#1222), a contributor wrote that "everything is based on models" and that observations
in the APIs are not "a viable option at the moment".

So the figures are model estimates, the past ones included.  Observations go into
each model run.  Whether Open-Meteo later revises a value for an interval that has
already passed is not known, and nothing here tested it.  A total built from those
values could therefore fall as well as rise.

## Today's daily sum includes the rest of the day

`daily=precipitation_sum` equalled the sum of all 24 hourly values for the local day,
the hours still to come included, in every location tried.  Twelve places were
queried with `timezone=auto`:

| place | local time | `precipitation_sum` | hours up to now | hours after now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ketchikan | 13:00 | 67.2 mm | 39.9 mm | 27.3 mm |
| Tokyo | 06:00 | 23.5 mm | 0.6 mm | 22.9 mm |
| Kuala Lumpur | 05:00 | 11.0 mm | 0.0 mm | 11.0 mm |
| Bogotá | 16:00 | 4.8 mm | 2.6 mm | 2.2 mm |

The "hours up to now" column included the value labeled 00:00, which belongs to the
day before, so it is slightly high.  The conclusion does not depend on it: the daily
sum is partly a forecast, and is not a total since midnight.

## Each value covers the interval that ends at its label

The documentation describes hourly `precipitation` as the "sum of the preceding hour"
and `minutely_15` as the "preceding 15 minutes sum".  The `current` block agrees.  At
14:25:59 Pacific it was labeled 14:15 with `"interval": 900`, and at 22:03:37 UTC it
was labeled 22:00.  So the current block covers the last completed quarter hour, and
a total since midnight is the sum of the values labeled after 00:00, up to and
including `current.time`.  The value labeled 00:00 belongs to the previous day.

`timeformat=unixtime` returns `current.time` as epoch seconds.  Without a `timezone`
parameter, times come back in GMT.

## Hourly and 15-minute values are separate products

The documentation says native 15-minute data comes from NOAA HRRR in North America
and from DWD ICON-D2 and Météo-France AROME in Central Europe.  Elsewhere "data is
interpolated from 1-hourly to 15-minutely."

The four 15-minute values for an hour did not add up to that hour's value, even where
the 15-minute data is native.  One request per place, `forecast_days=1`, about 3.4 KB
each:

| place | since midnight, hourly | since midnight, 15-minute | largest hour against its four quarters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ketchikan | 39.4 mm | 41.1 mm | 1.0 mm |
| Hilo | 16.2 mm | 16.1 mm | 2.4 mm |
| Tokyo | 0.6 mm | 0.7 mm | 0.6 mm |
| Bogotá | 2.6 mm | 2.9 mm | 0.2 mm |

The operator chose the 15-minute values.  A station never moves, so it always gets the
same kind of data, native or interpolated, and every comparison it makes is within
that kind.

## One request gives everything

`current`, `hourly` and `minutely_15` can all be asked for in the same request, and
every request above did so.  A total since midnight therefore costs no request beyond
the one the client already makes for each CSV row.

## OpenWeatherMap refreshes about every ten minutes, and changes less often

The free Current Weather API returns temperature, humidity, pressure, wind speed,
direction and gust, and `rain.1h` in mm/h, which is absent when it is not raining.  It
has no solar radiation and no total since midnight.  Its FAQ names "weather stations,
satellites, radar systems, and advanced models like GFS, ECMWF, and its own OWHL" as
sources, and does not say how they are combined.  The pricing page gives a refresh
rate for each paid plan, from "Every 2 hrs" to "Every 10 min", and none for the free
plan.

A poll ran every two minutes for 40 minutes, from 21:44 to 22:23 UTC, at Bangkok
(13.75, 100.50) in light rain.  The key belongs to the operator, who supplies station
data to OpenWeatherMap.  Their site says the account is on the free plan.

`dt` changed at 21:43:43, 21:54:36, 21:56:37, 22:06:41 and 22:16:45.  Every change
after the first matched the second of our request exactly, which fits a cache of
about ten minutes that the next request refills.  The values changed far less often
than `dt`:

- `rain.1h` read 0.54 for all 38 minutes.  Open-Meteo's 15-minute figure moved from
  0.5 mm to 0.6 mm in the same period.
- Temperature changed once, at 21:56, from 25.45 to 25.92.
- Gust went from 1.34 to 0.89 at 21:54 and back at 21:56.

So `dt` says when OpenWeatherMap last calculated its answer, not when its data last
changed.  The poll cannot say whether the rain was steady or `rain.1h` was stale.

## What this ruled out

- Open-Meteo's `precipitation_sum` as a total since midnight, because it contains
  forecast.
- Spreading the current hour across the minutes, because that counts forecast rain.
  The operator's rule is that a logged value describes only time that has passed.
- OpenWeatherMap for this release.  A total since midnight would have to come from
  integrating `rain.1h`, and nothing here shows that figure tracking real rain.
  `todo.md` says what would settle it.
