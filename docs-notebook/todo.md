## Faster sample rate support

The program runs at 16 kHz at the one station it was written for, and several pieces
were tuned there.

### The Tier-3a screening threshold does not transfer across sample rates

`ContinuousAnalyzer.FAST_SCAN_SNR` is 8.0 dB, measured at 16 kHz.  The statistic is
the ratio of the largest to the smallest score in the fit array, so a longer array
gives the extremes more chances and the noise-only floor rises with the sample rate.
Measured over 60 noise-only windows:

    rate     mean dB    max dB    fires on noise?
     8000       5.89      7.37    no
    16000       6.37      7.61    no
    32000       6.93      8.91    yes
    48000       7.05      8.74    yes

From about 32 kHz upward, noise alone crosses the threshold, so Tier 3a fires on every
call and escalates to the full FFT anyway.  It then costs a short FFT and saves
nothing, which is the same failure the threshold had at every rate when it was 4.0.

Deriving the threshold from the fit array's length would fix it.  What is missing is a
measurement of what a *real* signal scores at those rates to check a new threshold
against, since one that clears the noise and also rejects real arcs would be worse
than what is there now.  Full working is in `fast-scan-window.md`.

### Nothing has run above 16 kHz for long

The window sizes are pinned in time, and the Tier-3a window in pulse periods, so the
shapes are right at any rate.  What has not been done is running a station at 44.1 or
48 kHz for a few days and comparing what it logs against a 16 kHz one on the same
antenna.  Until then the higher rates are supported rather than proven.

## Measurement defects

### The silence sentinel reaches the CSV as a plausible SNR

`amplitude_to_dbm` returns `SILENCE_DBFS` of -128 for an amplitude of zero, and
`ContinuousAnalyzer` computes `snr = sig_dbm - noise_dbm` without knowing that.  A
noise floor of exactly -128.00 therefore writes an SNR about 128 dB above whatever the
signal read.  It happened for 104 rows in April 2026 at the one station running this.

Those rows look like measurements and are not.  Anything averaging a month of CSV sees
them, which is how they were found: a maximum-SNR scan returned 128 dB.  The fix is to
decide what a row means when the floor is the sentinel, and either refuse to write it
or mark it, rather than letting the arithmetic run.  See `sdr-gain-calibration.md`,
which uses the same scan for the arc headroom figure.

### The gain sweep has not met a quiet antenna in anger

Each of the three outcomes it can report is covered by a test against a simulated
receiver, and the ordinary one has been driven against a real broadband antenna.  The
two that say no gain works have only been reproduced from the shape the mag loop
measurements imply, never seen on hardware.

### The true gain per step is unmeasured

`[rtlsdr] audio_rf_conversion_db` starts at the negative of the nominal gain, because
the sweep measures differences and the chain constant cancels only in a difference.
Four proxies for the curve disagreed over the same span, from 0.99x to 1.36x, and
nothing on the band was strong enough to settle it.  A signal generator would.  The
figure of 57.5 dB against a nominal 49.6 in `config.py` and `sdr.py` is provisional,
neither confirmed nor refuted.  See `sdr-gain-calibration.md`.

## Setup program

### Tuner gain should be a picker, not a text box

The tuner accepts 29 fixed steps and `RtlSdrSource.set_gain` snaps anything else to
the nearest, so a typed 41.0 becomes 40.2 and the operator is never told.  CLAUDE.md's
rule is that a setting with a known set of acceptable values gets a picker.

What stops it is where the list lives.  The steps come from the hardware rather than
the schema, so the dialog has to open the receiver, and opening hardware from a
Textual worker is the `pilot.pause()` race `tools/slow_workers.py` exists for.  The
sweep already does it and its dialog is a working example, so the pattern is there to
copy.  A receiver that will not open needs an answer too: falling back to the text box
is probably right, since somebody has to be able to set a gain before the device is
working.

The same question applies to `device_index`, and the answer there was no.  See
`sdr-setup-program.md`: enumeration cannot tell two identical receivers apart, so a
picker would list two indistinguishable rows.  Gain is different, because the list is
exact and the snapping is invisible.

### Markdown is not covered by the subjectless-opener check

`ste_lint --fragments` reads Python and `schema.json`.  `markdown_prose` yields a line
at a time, so a wrapped paragraph arrives as halves of sentences and each half reads
as a fragment.  Joining a Markdown paragraph the way `_joined_blocks` joins a comment
run would close it.  Until then, `docs/` and `docs-notebook/` get the reading pass and
nothing mechanical.

Points the SDR pages need to make, as notes rather than draft prose:

- **Calibrate the gain when the band is quiet.**  A running arc raises the broadband
  floor, the sweep measures that as the band, and the gain comes out lower than it
  should be for the hours either side.  Observed: 28.0, then 19.7, then 25.4 dB across
  one evening as an intermittent arc came and went.  The reserve already covers the
  loud case, so the measurement wants the quiet one.  See
  `sdr-gain-calibration.md` for why the percentile cannot reject a raised floor.
- **Clipping applies across the whole sampled span**, not just the 4 kHz being
  measured, which is why the reserve is as large as it is.
- **Re-run the calibration after changing the tuner gain**, or carry the level
  calibration across by the difference.  Startup warns if the two disagree.
- **A quiet antenna cannot have both**, and the sweep gives up floor accuracy rather
  than headroom, saying how much it gave up.  Clipping cannot be undone and a floor
  that reads high can.
- **Remedies for clipping, in the order they cost least.**  Lowering the gain is the
  obvious one and the most expensive, because a step below the knee costs one to
  three decibels on every noise floor reported afterwards.  Cheaper first: move to a
  higher band, since powerline noise amplitude declines with frequency; or shift the
  listening frequency away from where the antenna is resonant, so it delivers less.
  An attenuator ahead of the receiver is the last resort and the only one that helps
  when the whole band is loud.

`docs/` needs the wording approved before anything is written to it, so these are for
the author rather than for a model to fill in unattended.

### PHASE_MOVE_MARGIN is a literal where a derivation would do

`ContinuousAnalyzer.PHASE_MOVE_MARGIN` is 1.05, commented as about 0.4 dB.  The dB
figure is the quantity somebody chose, so `10 ** (0.4 / 20)` would state it, but that
evaluates to 1.0471 and would move the value.  Small gain against real churn, so it is
recorded rather than done.  The comment could at least say 0.42 dB, which is what 1.05
actually is.
